# Iteration 3 — Independent panel review

Archive score: the lowest independent panel score, used as the conservative quality gate.

## Creative director

### Aesthetic read

A quiet botanical journal translated into a polished wellness storefront: warm paper, moss typography, restrained serif italics, and friendly printed objects. The strongest moments are the paired session offers and asymmetric resources section. It communicates the intended softness clearly, but its visual identity still draws heavily from familiar contemporary wellness conventions.

### Biggest gaps

1. **The hero illustration establishes the category more strongly than the brand.** The arch, peach sun, oversized leaves, winding path, and tilted affirmation note assemble nearly every expected botanical wellness motif. A studio execution would give one element a distinctive, memorable treatment and remove competing symbols.
2. **The reassuring language becomes repetitive across the whole composition.** “A little,” “soft,” “come back,” and “good things” recur alongside the same roman-to-italic headline switch. The cumulative effect feels carefully generated. Give practical sections more direct titles so the emotional phrases retain their impact.
3. **The shared focus menu feels like a compact information panel.** Its broad sage rectangle, fine rules, numbered circles, and small descriptions interrupt the more generous session presentation above. Increase the text’s presence and loosen the arrangement; this information deserves the same care as the pricing.
4. **The resources section has an unresolved lower-left void.** The book feature ends substantially before the wallpaper feature, leaving a large blank area without a compositional anchor. The asymmetric idea is good, but the book object needs more scale or vertical presence to make the imbalance feel intentional.
5. **The upcoming offers compress into a generic catalog.** Three evenly divided columns followed by two bordered previews create the page’s most conventional stretch. Tiny icons, labels, and repeated arrow links add detail without providing a clear editorial focal point.

### Highest-leverage move

Rebuild the hero around one unmistakable botanical artwork: enlarge and develop the main stem into a distinctive printed illustration, with richer shape and texture, then simplify the surrounding arch, landscape, note, and floating marks. The brand needs a signature image that can carry the otherwise disciplined page.

## UX design critic

### Aesthetic read

A warm botanical journal translated into a calm commercial page: ivory paper, moss panels, editorial serif type, and reassuring illustrations. The emotional tone is consistent and the paid offers receive appropriate prominence. A top studio would make the service itself equally legible; here, the atmosphere is more immediately understandable than what someone is booking.

### Biggest gaps

1. **The purchase journey lacks essential context.** Visitors reach $60 and $350 buttons without learning the session duration, delivery format, or who guides them. “Confirmed after checkout” moves practical uncertainty beyond the payment decision and weakens the welcoming premise.
2. **The shared focus menu arrives after the purchase controls.** Choosing a focus is central to understanding the personal reset, yet that explanation sits beneath both offers in a dense reference panel. The page asks for commitment before finishing the explanation.
3. **The resource section promises present value and delivers future availability.** “Free for you” suggests something visitors can use now, while both actions request launch notifications. The modest availability labels cannot correct the stronger headline quickly enough.
4. **Seven separate future interests dilute the second half.** Resources, three programs, retreats, and recommendations each ask for email interest before the general newsletter asks again. This creates an extended catalogue of unavailable things, making the business feel less established than the polished opening suggests.
5. **The reassuring vocabulary obscures useful distinctions.** “Your reset,” “The little things,” “What’s growing,” and repeated variations of “little,” “soft,” and “come back” require interpretation where navigation and offer labels should provide orientation. The consistent emotional register starts to flatten the hierarchy.

### Highest-leverage move

Rebuild the session section as one clear decision sequence: explain who guides the session, its format and duration, introduce the seven focuses, then present the two purchase options. This would turn the strongest visual section into a credible, understandable booking journey.

## Art director — visual system

### Aesthetic read

A quiet botanical journal translated into a wellness storefront: warm ivory, moss, literary serif headings, pastel paper objects, and reassuring copy. The palette is cohesive and the resource section has an appealing editorial asymmetry. It captures the brief’s softness, but a top studio would give this much more authorship; the repeated italic phrases, botanical symbols, and gentle cards currently feel familiar to the wellness category.

### Biggest gaps

1. **The hero illustration lacks a distinctive visual idea.** The enormous, densely stacked leaves overpower the small landscape, while the arch, peach sun, winding path, and tilted affirmation note assemble several familiar wellness motifs. Simplify the composition around one expressive botanical gesture and make the paper texture or drawing character visibly meaningful.
2. **The typography settles into one emotional register.** Nearly every section uses a compact serif heading with a sage italic continuation. Reserve that treatment for the hero and one later moment; vary heading proportions and line lengths so the page develops an editorial rhythm.
3. **The shared focus menu becomes a large administrative block.** Its broad gray-green rectangle, numerous rules, small numbered circles, and tightly packed descriptions interrupt the invitation established by the session cards. Give the list a lighter background treatment and stronger typographic distinction between session names and explanations.
4. **The upcoming section compresses five ideas into a catalogue.** Three closely matched columns followed immediately by two bordered previews make this area feel like a product-directory template. Establish one clear secondary feature and make the remaining announcements substantially quieter.
5. **The fine-detail system is overpopulated.** Eyebrows, outlined capsules, miniature symbols, numbered circles, arrow circles, and faint rules compete for attention throughout. Many are too delicate to contribute much at page scale. Reduce the vocabulary and give the surviving cues more presence.

### Highest-leverage move

Rebuild the hero artwork as a distinctive commissioned-looking botanical composition. One memorable image with a clear silhouette and tactile character would establish authorship that the existing palette and competent layout could carry through the page.

## Art director — motion and interaction

### Aesthetic read

A quiet botanical journal with a credible, gentle commercial purpose. The ivory, moss, serif typography, and asymmetric resource section express the direction well. The visible interaction language feels considerably more conventional than the illustration: arrows, pills, and small underlined links provide utility but little personality. A still screenshot cannot establish animation or scroll quality.

### Biggest gaps

1. **The hero offers no distinctive invitation to move through the page.** Its isolated caption beneath the artwork is barely perceptible, while the full-width values strip acts as a visual stop. Connect the winding illustrated path to a legible, understated “Explore your reset” scroll cue.
2. **Secondary actions are too visually recessive.** “Notify me,” “Keep me posted,” and the retreat links sit in small, low-contrast text beside tiny circled symbols. Increase their visual presence and give the circle-and-label combination a clearer shared hit-area silhouette.
3. **The lower page loses its editorial cadence.** Three program columns followed immediately by two bordered previews create a directory-like sequence of equally quiet interaction targets. Introduce a more generous pause and stronger compositional change before the retreat and recommendations.
4. **The hero’s potential animation hierarchy is visually unresolved.** The dense central foliage dominates the arch, sun, path, and note; the scattered plus and dots appear incidental. Simplify the peripheral marks and give the note and path clearer spatial roles so any restrained entrance sequence has an intelligible focal order.

### Highest-leverage move

Make the hero’s winding path lead visibly toward the session section through one purposeful scroll invitation. That would unite artwork, navigation, and the brand’s idea of gently returning to yourself.
