# Iteration 1 — Independent panel review

Archive score: the lowest independent panel score, used as the conservative quality gate.

## Creative director

### Aesthetic read

A coherent botanical wellness journal: ivory paper, moss ink, gentle serif italics, and pastel illustrations establish the intended calm immediately. It feels considered and welcoming, but the execution still resembles a polished wellness template. A top studio would give the brand a more distinctive visual signature, stronger editorial pacing, and greater confidence in what deserves attention.

### Biggest gaps

1. **The session composition breaks the page’s balance.** The dark seven-day card extends far below its pale companion, leaving a conspicuous empty rectangle on the left. Its seven tightly ruled entries read like administrative detail inside what should be the page’s clearest invitation.
2. **The visual vocabulary is too familiar.** The arch, peach sun, oversized leaves, tilted affirmation note, sprouts, and scattered sparkles collectively reproduce a ubiquitous wellness aesthetic. The hero needs one memorable authored gesture; adding familiar motifs does not create one.
3. **The lower page becomes a catalog of boxes.** Two resource cards lead into three program cards and then two preview cards, all using similar borders, labels, and arrow links. The promised editorial rhythm flattens into inventory, with future offerings consuming substantial visual attention.
4. **Too much practical information retreats into tiny, pale text.** Session descriptions, card metadata, supporting paragraphs, and newsletter notes have noticeably less presence than the headlines. Softness becomes timidity; premium editorial work would preserve quietness while making the reading hierarchy more decisive.
5. **Repeated lyrical treatment dilutes its effect.** Nearly every section combines a small uppercase eyebrow with a two-line serif heading and italic reassurance. The repeated “little,” “soft,” and “come back” language makes each section feel less specific. The visible red “1 Issue” badge also punctures the finished presentation.

### Highest-leverage move

Recompose the paid sessions as one deliberate editorial spread: align the two offers and their purchase actions, then give the seven focuses a shared, spacious area below. This would repair the largest compositional defect and turn the primary business section into the page’s most confident moment.

## UX design critic

### Aesthetic read

A quiet botanical wellness journal: ivory paper, moss ink, gentle serif headlines, and illustrated keepsakes. The visual language is coherent and approachable, but the journey feels like a polished catalog of soft promises. A top studio would preserve this calm while making the actual service, decision, and next step considerably clearer.

### Biggest gaps

1. **The page asks for a purchase before explaining the experience.** Neither the hero nor session cards clearly establishes what happens in a session, how long it lasts, or how it is delivered. “Find your reset” creates atmosphere but provides little orientation for a first visit.
2. **The session comparison is structurally lopsided.** The seven-day card contains the full focus list and extends far below the single-session card. Those focuses also inform the single-session choice, yet their placement makes them appear exclusive to the package. Uneven card heights and separated buttons make comparison harder.
3. **Future offerings occupy too much of the journey.** Seven unavailable concepts receive individual cards and competing interest actions. This creates a long storefront whose apparent breadth exceeds what visitors can actually buy. The primary booking path loses momentum after its first appearance.
4. **Reassurance repeats while useful information stays scarce.** “A little,” “come back,” “good,” and “soft” recur across section titles and descriptions, making distinct destinations feel interchangeable. Navigation such as “The little things” compounds the ambiguity. The warmth needs more concrete nouns and outcomes.
5. **The fine-print hierarchy undermines the invitation.** Session descriptions, availability badges, and small text links are visually faint beside abundant empty space. The red “1 Issue” overlay also interrupts the carefully composed page and visibly compromises its finish.

### Highest-leverage move

Rebuild the session section around one clear explanation of the experience and one shared focus selector, followed by two compact, aligned purchase options. This would clarify the service, repair the comparison, and give the page a confident primary destination.

## Art director — visual system

### Aesthetic read

A muted botanical journal: warm ivory, moss, gentle serif italics, and paper-like illustrations deliver the intended calm, approachable wellness mood. The palette is coherent and the hero has presence. But the execution feels closer to a polished wellness template than an independent studio identity: repeated heading formulas, familiar arch-and-leaf imagery, and relentlessly tidy cards dilute the promised personal, slightly imperfect character.

### Biggest gaps

1. **The session pair breaks the page’s composition.** The seven-day card extends far below the personal-reset card, leaving an enormous vacant rectangle beneath the primary entry offer. It makes the section look unresolved and disproportionately dominated by package details. Give the two offers a deliberate shared structure and move the seven-focus catalogue into a separate shared area.
2. **The hero artwork is recognizable as a category cliché before it is recognizable as Shin.** The arch, peach sun, layered hills, oversized leaves, and tilted affirmation combine too many familiar wellness motifs. Simplify around one distinctive botanical gesture, with more convincing print texture and less diagrammatic geometry.
3. **Every major section repeats the same editorial trick.** Upright serif statement, sage italic second line, tiny uppercase eyebrow, and explanatory copy on the right recur so consistently that the page feels assembled. Keep that treatment for the hero and one meaningful return; vary the subsequent typography and alignment to create actual editorial pacing.
4. **The lower page becomes a catalogue of faint boxes.** Three program cards followed by two preview cards create a dense, uniform product directory. The creative direction calls for quiet anticipation, but this arrangement gives every future idea its own competing container. Use one open typographic program list and one larger illustrated retreat feature to give the content different weights.
5. **The fine print is too visually recessive.** Focus descriptions, category labels, availability pills, and card actions become miniature grey texture against already muted surfaces. Increase supporting-text contrast and size, remove redundant micro-labels, and let important actions occupy clearer visual space. The visible red “1 Issue” badge also undermines the otherwise calm finish.

### Highest-leverage move

Recompose the paid-session section into two compact, aligned offer cards with a shared seven-focus area beneath. This removes the largest compositional dead zone, makes the booking choice easier to scan, and establishes a stronger rhythm for the rest of the page.

## Art director — motion and interaction

### Aesthetic read

A soft botanical journal translated into an orderly storefront: ivory paper, moss ink, serif reassurance, and gently tilted keepsakes. The restraint fits the brief, but the visible interaction language feels conventional and repetitive. A studio execution would make the journey feel deliberately paced and personal; this screenshot reads as a succession of polite cards. Animation, hover, and scrolling behavior cannot be verified from a still.

### Biggest gaps

1. **Every destination receives nearly the same arrow treatment.** Purchases, signup invitations, and editorial links use small diagonal arrows despite producing different outcomes. Give section navigation, checkout, and interest signup distinct, consistent cues so the experience feels intentional before interaction.
2. **The session section loses its rhythm halfway down.** The seven-day card extends far below the single-session card, leaving a conspicuous blank pocket. Its seven tightly ruled entries feel administrative beside the invitation above; condense that detail and restore a composed pair.
3. **The hero has atmosphere but little directional tension.** The large arch and oversized leaves create a static block, while the floating note feels attached as decoration. Recompose the path and note to carry the eye toward the primary action; the artwork should help begin the journey.
4. **The long scroll repeats its invitation too predictably.** Section eyebrow, italic heading, right-hand paragraph, cards, arrows: the repetition makes calm feel templated. Give one middle section a more intimate, editorial composition to create a meaningful change in pace.
5. **The red “1 Issue” badge visibly breaks the world.** Its saturated color and utility styling interrupt the values strip transition. Remove it from the presented experience before judging production finish.

### Highest-leverage move

Rebuild the session pair around equal visual weight and a shared purchase baseline, reducing the seven-day details to a concise overview with an explicit expansion cue. This would repair the page’s biggest interruption in scroll rhythm and make its primary decision clearer.
