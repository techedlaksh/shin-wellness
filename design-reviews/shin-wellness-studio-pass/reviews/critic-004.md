# Iteration 4 — Independent panel review

Archive score: the lowest independent panel score, used as the conservative quality gate.

## Creative director

### Aesthetic read

A warm botanical journal translated into a composed wellness storefront. The ivory, sage, moss, and lilac palette feels coherent; the oversized arch and generous hero establish calm immediately. The resource section adds welcome editorial asymmetry. It is polished and approachable, but its repeated soft-serif formulas and familiar botanical imagery still feel closer to a well-designed wellness template than a distinctive studio identity.

### Biggest gaps

1. **The visual signature is too familiar.** An arch, peach sun, rolling hills, and oversized sprig communicate wellness instantly but reveal little about Shin. Give the illustration a more particular drawing language; the current geometric smoothness weakens the promised handwritten, personal character.
2. **Too many headings use the same emotional cadence.** Upright serif followed by a sage italic line repeats through the hero, sessions, resources, programs, and newsletter. Reserve that treatment for two defining moments so the page develops rather than continually restating its opening gesture.
3. **The resource composition leaves a conspicuous hole.** The reading feature ends well above the wallpaper entry, leaving a large unused rectangle beneath the left column. The asymmetry is promising, but the imbalance feels unresolved. Extend the reading artwork or deliberately align the two entries’ closing actions.
4. **The practical layer is visually timid.** Pale metadata, fine borders, miniature focus descriptions, and restrained text links blur together at page scale. Increase contrast and reduce incidental labels so useful information has the same care and confidence as the headlines.
5. **The lower page becomes a waiting-list catalogue.** Three program columns followed by two preview cards create five nearly equal invitations with repeated small actions. Compress the secondary previews into a quieter editorial line and give the three primary programs more breathing room.

### Highest-leverage move

Develop one unmistakably Shin illustration language and carry it from the hero into the resource artwork. A more expressive, imperfect botanical drawing system would deliver the personal warmth promised by the direction and make this cohesive page memorable.

## UX design critic

### Aesthetic read

A quiet botanical journal with a convincing editorial rhythm: warm paper, moss typography, generous pauses, and an inviting illustrated landscape. The asymmetric resource section adds welcome character. The experience feels considered, but its poetic language and numerous future offerings leave the practical booking journey less resolved than the visual identity.

### Biggest gaps

1. **The service remains unclear when purchase is requested.** Neither the hero nor the pricing cards plainly explains what a session involves, its duration, or how it happens. The $60 and $350 commitments arrive before visitors can confidently picture the experience.
2. **The decision sequence runs backward.** “Buy a single session” appears above the shared focus menu, although choosing a focus is central to understanding that purchase. Introduce the menu before—or directly within—the choice between one session and seven.
3. **The apparent free-resource destination is actually another waitlist.** “Good for your day. Free for you.” promises something immediately useful; both actions defer access. Make availability explicit in the section introduction so the secondary journey delivers the expectation it creates.
4. **Future interests accumulate into a competing sitemap.** Two resources, three programs, retreats, recommendations, and a general newsletter produce eight closely related signup invitations. The lower page becomes an inventory of possibilities without helping visitors prioritize one.
5. **Practical interaction cues are visually subordinate to atmosphere.** Tiny availability labels, pale inline actions, and understated arrows make it harder to distinguish something bookable from something forthcoming. Give status and action text enough contrast and scale to resolve that distinction at a glance.

### Highest-leverage move

Rebuild the session section as one clear decision sequence: explain the session experience, show the seven focuses, then compare and book the two packages. That would turn the page’s strongest visual destination into an equally strong user journey.

## Art director — visual system

### Aesthetic read

A calm botanical journal translated into a polished wellness storefront: warm paper tones, moss typography, generous margins, and restrained illustration. The paired session offers establish a clear commercial center, while the asymmetric resource section introduces welcome editorial variety. It captures the supplied direction convincingly, but its illustration vocabulary and repeated typographic gestures still feel familiar rather than unmistakably Shin.

### Biggest gaps

1. **The hero artwork lacks a distinctive artistic voice.** The arch, layered hills, peach sun, and enlarged sprig form an exceptionally familiar wellness composition. Keep those ingredients, but give them more expressive drawing, irregular contours, and visibly considered print texture. The current finish feels closer to a tidy vector asset than an artwork commissioned for the brand.
2. **Too many headlines use the same emotional cadence.** “Yourself again,” “a little reset,” “Free for you,” “come back to you,” and “more wellbeing” repeat the serif-plus-sage-italic treatment. Reserve that signature for the hero and one later moment; let other sections gain identity through different scale, line breaks, and placement.
3. **The shared focus menu becomes a broad slab of low-contrast detail.** Its muted background, fine rules, tiny numbering, and closely ranked text flatten seven distinct choices into one administrative block. Strengthen the focus names, simplify the subordinate information, and give the section more internal whitespace.
4. **The upcoming-offerings area feels like a component catalog.** Three equal columns followed by two bordered rectangles compress the brand into a predictable collection of modules. The tiny icons and repeated metadata do little to distinguish the experiences. A more editorial hierarchy would preserve its secondary role while making this section feel authored.
5. **Small interface details are overly timid.** Eyebrows, supporting copy, outlined pills, and several text links recede together. The dark session card demonstrates that stronger contrast can remain gentle; apply that confidence selectively to the practical information elsewhere.

### Highest-leverage move

Commission a more distinctive illustration language, starting with the hero and extending it into the resource artwork. Preserve the botanical concept, but make the drawing, texture, and composition specific enough that the page could be recognized without its wordmark.

## Art director — motion and interaction

### Aesthetic read

A warm botanical journal with a clear booking hierarchy and an appealingly quiet voice. The asymmetric resource section supplies the most distinctive rhythm. From this still, however, the interaction language feels more conventional than the illustration: small arrows, pills, and underlined links carry nearly every invitation. Animation quality and actual scroll behavior cannot be assessed from this screenshot.

### Biggest gaps

1. **The hero’s strongest interaction idea is nearly invisible.** “Follow the path / explore your reset” sits beneath the illustration at annotation scale. Connecting that invitation visibly to the illustrated path would make the artwork feel integral to the visitor’s journey.
2. **Arrow conventions lack clear meaning.** Diagonal arrows accompany booking and contact actions, while circled arrows accompany notifications and previews. The treatment distinguishes styles more clearly than outcomes; opening a signup dialog deserves a consistent, recognizable cue.
3. **The lower page repeats the same interaction beat.** Three program panels and two preview panels each finish with a small text action and circled arrow. Five comparable invitations create a catalog rhythm where the direction calls for a personal, unhurried progression.
4. **The shared focus menu looks ambiguously interactive.** Numbered circles, rules, and individually grouped descriptions suggest selectable rows, but no visible instruction clarifies whether visitors can choose there. Make its informational or selectable role unmistakable.
5. **Delicacy sometimes becomes hesitation.** Resource signup links and the hero’s secondary action have little visual presence. Slightly stronger text contrast and a more deliberate underline would preserve softness while making action feel confident.

### Highest-leverage move

Turn the hero’s illustrated path into a clear visual invitation to the session section: strengthen and position its existing scroll cue so the path, label, and destination read as one designed gesture.
