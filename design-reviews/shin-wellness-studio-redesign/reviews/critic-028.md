Aesthetic read: A convincing, restrained wellness editorial with warm paper tones, handsome serif typography, and clear emphasis on paid sessions. The overlapping book and phone compositions bring welcome personality. It feels carefully designed, but the immaculate surfaces and repeated typographic formula stop it short of the tactile, distinctive character of a top studio.

Biggest gaps:

1. **The hero illustration lacks authorship.** A thin botanical stem, peach disc, and sweeping arcs read as familiar wellness ingredients assembled together. Commission a more characterful botanical composition with irregular contours, expressive cropping, and subtle material texture.
2. **The hero’s composition is too cleanly divided.** The headline occupies the left and the isolated flower occupies the right, with a large inactive gulf between them. Introduce one deliberate overlap or spatial interruption that makes the opening feel composed specifically for this brand.
3. **The typography repeats its signature too predictably.** Large moss serif headings, occasional italic words, and miniature uppercase labels recur throughout. Establish more distinct section voices through scale, measure, and placement; reserve the italic treatment for a genuinely memorable moment.
4. **The paid offers feel like a polished pricing table.** The hard rectangular division, prominent dollar amounts, rules, and oversized ghosted “7” lean toward financial-product design. Give the session folio a more personal editorial composition, with more emphasis on the experience and less decorative numerology.
5. **The lower page loses compositional momentum.** “More ways to come back to you” becomes a tall left column beside two flat lilac slabs, immediately followed by another conventional two-column section. Turn the upcoming material into one compact, unmistakably handmade studio note to create a stronger transition into the closing invitation.
6. **Too much supporting information retreats into faint microtype.** Descriptions, status labels, and closing annotations look substantially weaker than the generous headlines. Increase their size and contrast selectively so the quiet mood still carries readable, confident information.

Highest-leverage move: Rebuild the hero around a distinctive, tactile botanical artwork that interacts with the typography and gives the entire brand a recognizable visual signature.
