Score: 7.6/10

Aesthetic read: Warm, coherent, and pleasantly unhurried; the botanical hero and moss pricing section establish a convincing identity. It feels like a polished wellness template more than an unmistakable studio commission. The handmade gestures mostly decorate an otherwise conventional sequence of horizontal sections.

Biggest gaps:

1. **The paid-offer composition feels mechanically stretched.** The single-session paper has a large empty lower half, while the seven-day offer accumulates tiny subdivisions. Give the two offers deliberate, independent proportions; make the package’s value and purchase action command the panel.
2. **Too much essential information becomes visual dust.** Offer explanations, itinerary entries, captions, and labels are consistently miniature against oversized headings. Increase reading text and reduce supporting copy until the page feels generous at ordinary viewing distance.
3. **The section rhythm is too predictable.** Cream hero, cream pricing, sage freebies, peach announcement, moss newsletter form a stack of full-width color bands. Introduce one meaningful compositional interruption through overlapping paper, an offset illustration, or an editorial arrangement that crosses a section boundary.
4. **The hero illustration carries scale without enough character.** The giant flower, thin orbit, peach circle, and diagonal arc read as assembled wellness motifs. Develop one more distinctive botanical drawing with richer line variation and a clearer relationship to the headline; remove incidental geometry.
5. **The free-resource mockups lean on familiar template shorthand.** Floating pastel books and reminder cards with soft shadows feel like stock product renders. Make these tangible editorial objects through more specific typography, credible paper detail, and a less evenly staged arrangement.
6. **The upcoming offer looks like a UI placeholder.** Its flat lilac rectangle and divided lower row feel administrative beside the expressive hero. Treat it as an actual note in progress, with a stronger typographic gesture and a more intentional offset.

Highest-leverage move: Recompose the paid-session section into a confident editorial centerpiece with independently proportioned offers, substantially larger reading text, and one unmistakable primary purchase action.
