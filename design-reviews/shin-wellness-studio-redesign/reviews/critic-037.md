Score: 7.6/10

Aesthetic read: A convincing, quietly premium wellness identity, with a lovely botanical hero and restrained color palette. The page feels considerate, but its strongest handmade gestures are concentrated in the illustration and free-resource mockups; the commercial experience feels substantially more conventional. Repeated fine rules, miniature labels, and soft editorial headlines start to resemble a template.

Biggest gaps:
1. **The paid offers lose the brand’s personality.** Two large rectangular price panels introduce a rigid sales comparison immediately after the expressive hero. Build one distinctive session folio with unequal offer proportions, more deliberate typography, and a tactile detail that makes purchasing feel part of this particular brand.
2. **The lilac upcoming-offers sheet overwhelms the lower composition.** Its oversized, right-heavy overlap leaves a broad vacant area beside it and competes with the established offers. Reduce its footprint and give it a clear compositional relationship to the two free resources.
3. **Secondary text becomes visual dust.** Descriptions, price qualifiers, availability labels, and button captions are consistently tiny and low contrast. Increase their size and selectively remove microcopy so the spaciousness supports comfortable reading.
4. **The free resources receive the most seductive product presentation.** Dimensional books and phones have more material richness than the paid sessions. Give the paid experience an equally compelling visual expression while simplifying the speculative resource previews.
5. **Too many sections share the same editorial formula.** Small uppercase eyebrow, large serif headline, hairline divider, muted supporting text: the repetition flattens the journey. Introduce one intimate, differently scaled moment—a short note or focused session vignette—to interrupt the catalog rhythm.
6. **Decorative marks accumulate without sufficient purpose.** Faint arcs, corner botanicals, scattered sprouts, and the folded lilac corner collectively feel applied. Retain fewer, more distinctive gestures and connect them to content or transitions.

Highest-leverage move: Redesign the paid-session area as the page’s signature editorial object, giving the core experience the visual character and material appeal currently reserved for the free-resource mockups.
