Aesthetic read: Warm, assured, and convincingly gentle; the moss pricing panel, restrained palette, and botanical hero establish a clear identity. It feels closer to a polished wellness template than an unmistakable studio commission because the handmade qualities remain decorative while most of the page follows familiar editorial patterns.

Biggest gaps:
1. **The lower page loses compositional conviction.** “More ways to come back to you” combines a large lilac tile with a cramped, overlapping stack of small offers, leaving awkward unused space below the tile. Turn this into a deliberately composed journal spread with fewer competing alignments and a clearer relationship between the featured offer and supporting entries.
2. **The handmade language feels applied rather than authored.** Faint arcs, isolated sprouts, small stars, and loose flourishes recur without a strong visual purpose. Replace several of these with one distinctive illustrated system whose shapes actively frame content and guide the eye.
3. **Secondary information becomes excessively small and faint.** Offer descriptions, category labels, pricing qualifications, and tertiary links retreat into near-illegibility. Increase their size and contrast, then simplify the copy where necessary to preserve spaciousness.
4. **The free-resource mockups look generic beside the stronger botanical hero.** Floating pastel books, phone cards, and a broad blurred shadow resemble standard promotional mockups. Give these resources distinctive cover artwork and present them as a more tactile, intentionally arranged still life.
5. **The typography repeats its signature too predictably.** Large serif headings with sage or peach emphasis appear throughout, making successive sections feel interchangeable. Establish a more varied editorial rhythm through changes in scale, line length, and placement, reserving colored emphasis for a few meaningful moments.
6. **The slogan strip interrupts the otherwise quiet pacing.** Three evenly distributed affirmations read like a conventional marketing reassurance bar. Replace it with a single memorable transitional note that feels individually placed and allows the hero to flow into the paid offering.

Highest-leverage move: Recompose the free and upcoming sections into one distinctive journal-like sequence, using stronger typography and purposeful asymmetry to replace the current assortment of promotional tiles.
