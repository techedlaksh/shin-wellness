1. Score: 7.8/10

2. Aesthetic read: Warm, restrained, and recognizably editorial; the moss, ivory, and botanical language already belong together. The strongest moments are the generous hero and the tangible book-and-note illustrations. It still feels more like a polished wellness template than a distinctive studio identity, particularly through the lower half.

3. Biggest gaps:
   1. **The page becomes a stack of color-coded sections.** Sage resources, peach upcoming offers, and moss newsletter form predictable horizontal bands; introduce one consequential overlap or offset composition that connects sections and gives the page a less assembled rhythm.
   2. **The free resources receive the most evocative product presentation.** Books and wallpapers feel tangible and desirable, while paid sessions rely on prices and compact lists; give the paid offer an equally compelling visual expression of the session experience.
   3. **The oversized hero flower lacks the refinement its prominence demands.** Repeated leaf shapes, sketch strokes, floating peach circle, and faint enclosing arc feel like assembled decorative ingredients; commission a more distinctive botanical gesture with deliberate line variation and fewer supporting devices.
   4. **Too much secondary copy is visually miniature.** Offer details, resource labels, accordion rows, and reassurance text become fine print against expansive whitespace; increase their size and contrast, and remove low-value annotations to preserve calm.
   5. **The seven-day offer crowds several competing treatments into one rectangle.** Large price, explanatory copy, tilted paper note, pill button, and footer caption weaken the hierarchy; simplify the card around one clear promise, one experiential detail, and one decisive action.
   6. **The final third loses compositional ambition.** The lilac announcement rectangle and conventional newsletter row feel resolved by component placement; develop a more personal closing composition through a larger editorial note and an intentionally integrated signup.

4. Highest-leverage move: Redesign the paid-session section as the page’s signature editorial composition, giving the actual experience the visual presence currently reserved for the free downloads.
