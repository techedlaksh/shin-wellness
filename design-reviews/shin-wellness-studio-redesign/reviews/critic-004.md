But the page feels carefully assembled from a wellness brand kit: consistently tasteful, insufficiently distinctive. Its handmade gestures are decorative rather than expressive.

Biggest gaps:

1. **Secondary text is excessively small and faint.** Navigation, offer descriptions, labels, and footer copy recede into texture. Increase their size and contrast, reduce unnecessary microcopy, and give practical information the same consideration as headlines.
2. **The hero illustration feels like generic geometric wellness artwork.** The oversized leaf stem, arch, peach sun, and winding path combine familiar symbols without a memorable signature. Commission one more particular, asymmetrical botanical composition with a warmer line quality and less mechanically perfect geometry.
3. **The same typographic recipe repeats throughout.** Large moss serif headlines repeatedly switch to sage mid-sentence, while tiny uppercase eyebrows introduce each section. Reserve these treatments for a few meaningful moments and introduce stronger variation in scale, alignment, and editorial pacing.
4. **The upcoming-offers area becomes a miniature dashboard.** Numbered entries, separators, status labels, two-column subdivisions, and illustrated cards create too many competing organizational devices. Consolidate this into a simpler field-note composition with one featured idea and clearly subordinate entries.
5. **The free-resource collage promises tactility but delivers flat rectangles.** The overlapping cream, sage, and lilac panels and tilted books resemble stock mockups; their material character barely registers. Give this section one convincing paper object, deliberate cropping, and a more generous relationship between illustration and text.
6. **The visible red “1 Issue” badge breaks the atmosphere.** It sits conspicuously above the reassurance strip. Remove development interface artifacts before presenting the page.

Highest-leverage move: Rebuild the typography hierarchy around comfortably readable supporting copy and more selectively expressive headlines; this would immediately make the entire page feel more assured, accessible, and carefully art-directed.
