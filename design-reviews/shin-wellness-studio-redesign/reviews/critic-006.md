Aesthetic read: Warm, composed, and recognizably premium, with a convincing moss-and-ivory palette and strong emphasis on paid sessions. The free-resource collage brings welcome personality, but the overall experience still feels more like an accomplished wellness template than a distinctive, personal journal. Its restraint occasionally becomes timidity: too much tiny copy, too little expressive variation.

Biggest gaps:

1. **The hero illustration feels assembled from familiar vector ingredients.** The peach disc, arched landscape, winding path, and oversized branch communicate generic wellness. Develop one unmistakable botanical artwork with more observational detail, irregular edges, and a stronger relationship to the headline.

2. **Supporting typography is consistently too small and faint.** Navigation, descriptions, service details, and card labels become a pale secondary texture across the page. Increase their size and contrast, shorten copy where needed, and create clearer intermediate type sizes between monumental headings and miniature annotations.

3. **The composition settles into repeated left-title/right-content arrangements.** The services introduction, free resources, upcoming offers, and newsletter all revisit this familiar balance. Introduce one decisive compositional interruption—a full-width editorial passage or a generously isolated paper note—to make the page feel authored.

4. **The free-resource collage looks art-directed but mechanically layered.** Large rectangular backgrounds, uniformly neat mockups, and overlapping information cards compete for attention. Build a more believable tabletop composition with fewer objects, tactile paper surfaces, and one clear foreground message.

5. **The upcoming section becomes a dense miniature product directory.** Five offers, several rules, small captions, and two tiny illustrations dilute the calm established above. Feature one upcoming idea generously and compress the remaining previews into a simpler, quieter list.

6. **Handmade character lives mostly in scattered decorative marks.** Tiny sprouts, curved strokes, and stars feel applied after the layout rather than intrinsic to it. Give one recurring mark a specific visual role and carry its irregularity into framing, illustration, and spacing.

Highest-leverage move: Replace the generic hero artwork with a distinctive, tactile botanical illustration that establishes a visual language the rest of the page can inherit.
