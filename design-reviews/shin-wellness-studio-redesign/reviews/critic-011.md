Aesthetic read: Warm, thoughtful, and recognizably Shin—the botanical hero, paper textures, and moss palette establish a convincing quiet-journal mood. The strongest moments feel art directed; the lower half becomes a small-type product catalogue, and the recurring wellness language begins to feel manufactured.

Biggest gaps:
1. **Typography loses authority below the headlines.** Descriptions, labels, prices’ qualifiers, and links are visibly tiny against generous empty space. Increase supporting type and reduce copy so the calm comes from legibility and restraint.
2. **The upcoming section breaks into too many competing miniature offers.** Its banner, paired columns, illustrations, labels, and repeated links resemble a compact dashboard. Give one future offer a deliberate editorial feature and consolidate the remainder into a quieter index.
3. **The paid cards’ overlap feels compositionally unresolved.** The oversized tilted moss card floats above the pale single-session card, with several backing-paper edges adding noise. Establish a clearer shared baseline and use one purposeful offset to express the handmade character.
4. **The free section lacks one coherent visual scene.** Floating books, isolated phone mockups, pastel panels, and detached shadows read as assembled promotional assets. Unite these into one tactile still life with consistent lighting, scale, and grounding.
5. **The verbal and typographic cadence repeats excessively.** “A little,” “good,” “your,” and “come back” recur across similarly weighted serif statements. Sharpen the section identities and reserve the most expressive serif treatment for a few memorable moments.
6. **The palette becomes overwhelmingly ivory and sage.** Peach and lilac remain confined to small decorative objects, so the long page has limited emotional progression. Introduce one substantial, carefully muted color field to distinguish the free or forthcoming chapter.

Highest-leverage move: Recompose the lower half into fewer, larger editorial moments with readable supporting text and a sharply reduced upcoming-offer grid.
