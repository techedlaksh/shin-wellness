Biggest gaps:

1. **The page repeats the same editorial introduction too often.** Eyebrow, large serif heading, small paragraph to the right, then cards recur through the middle. Break that rhythm with one intimate, generously typeset note or a markedly different composition.
2. **The hero botanical has scale without enough character.** Its uniform outlines, repeated leaf shapes, and loosely related peach strokes read as generic vector decoration. Commission a more expressive illustration with irregular line weight, considered negative space, and fewer, purposeful surrounding marks.
3. **Supporting text is consistently too timid.** Navigation, descriptions, labels, and action links become faint texture beneath the headlines. Increase the size and contrast of meaningful copy, and reserve miniature lettering for genuinely secondary annotations.
4. **The upcoming section’s overlap feels obstructive.** The cream list panel crowds the lilac card’s right edge and leaves its copy visually squeezed. Give the lilac feature a clean reading area; let only a blank paper margin overlap.
5. **The seven-day card dominates through sheer dark mass.** Its large moss rectangle, oversized price, and ghosted numeral pull attention away from the gentler single-session invitation. Preserve its premium emphasis with proportion and typography while reducing the block’s visual heaviness.
6. **The handmade details lack a consistent visual vocabulary.** Dotted arcs, curved flourishes, sprouts, tilted paper, and decorative rules accumulate without a clear relationship. Develop two or three distinctive gestures and repeat them with deliberate restraint.

Highest-leverage move: Replace one repeated heading-and-cards section with a beautifully typeset journal-note composition that gives the page a memorable change of rhythm and a more personal voice.
